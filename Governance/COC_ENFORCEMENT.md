# Code of Conduct Enforcement

## Purpose and Scope

This policy defines a predictable, proportionate response when a participant
violates the TeachLink Mobile Code of Conduct. It applies to project spaces and
activities, including repository discussions, events, and direct project-related
communications. It complements, and does not replace, the
[Conflict Resolution and Code of Conduct Appeals Process](processes/CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md).

## Principles

- Respond to reported behavior, not a person's status, identity, or viewpoint.
- Consider the impact, context, intent where reasonably knowable, prior conduct,
  and the participant's response. Good-faith disagreement or criticism is not by
  itself a Code of Conduct violation.
- Use the least restrictive response that reasonably protects participants and
  restores a constructive environment. Repetition or increased severity may
  justify moving to a higher step.
- Keep reports and deliberations private to the people who need them. Share a
  concise, anonymized summary of outcomes when doing so does not expose affected
  people or compromise safety.
- Do not retaliate against anyone for raising a concern, assisting with a review,
  or appealing a decision.

## Enforcement Authority

The community lead or a maintainer designated as a conduct responder may receive
reports, assess them, and apply this policy. A responder must disclose a conflict
and take no part in a decision where they are involved in the incident or have a
material stake in its outcome. The remaining maintainers appoint an uninvolved
responder; if none is available, they may appoint a neutral external facilitator.

Where practical, at least two uninvolved responders review a formal warning,
restriction, suspension, or ban. If immediate action is necessary to protect
someone or stop ongoing harm, one responder may temporarily restrict access while
that review is arranged. A second uninvolved maintainer reviews the temporary
action as soon as practicable, normally within 48 hours. Interim restrictions are
not a finding that a violation occurred.

## Graduated Enforcement Steps

The responder selects a step based on the criteria below. The steps are a guide,
not a mandatory sequence: severe conduct may require immediate action, and a
report with insufficient information may instead be closed or handled informally.

| Step | Response | Criteria and application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Private reminder | A private, specific request to stop or correct the behavior, with the relevant expectation explained. | Appropriate for an isolated, low-impact incident, an apparent misunderstanding, or a minor first violation. The responder contacts the participant privately and may offer a constructive way to repair the impact. |
| 2. Formal warning | A written notice describing the behavior, required change, and likely next step if it recurs. | Appropriate when a minor violation continues after a reminder, or when a single moderate violation warrants a clear documented boundary. The conduct responders review the notice; it is delivered privately and retained in the restricted conduct record. |
| 3. Temporary participation restriction | A time-limited restriction on the relevant project spaces or interactions, normally 7 to 30 days, with its scope and end date stated. | Appropriate for repeated violations after a warning, a substantial disruption, or conduct that needs a cooling-off period to protect participants. Responders specify the affected spaces and permitted essential contact, if any, and review the restriction before it expires. |
| 4. Suspension | Removal from project participation for a stated period, with access restored only under stated conditions, if any. | Appropriate for repeated or serious violations, including targeted harassment, persistent unwanted contact, or evasion of a prior restriction. Responders state the duration, scope, and conditions for return in the decision notice. |
| 5. Permanent exclusion | A ban from project spaces and activities, including removal of access and moderation of attempts to return. | Appropriate for severe conduct such as credible threats, publishing private identifying information without consent, or persistent serious violations despite prior action. Responders document why a lesser measure would not adequately protect participants. |

Conduct involving an immediate safety risk may receive an interim restriction or
move directly to suspension or permanent exclusion while facts are assessed. The
decision must still be reviewed by uninvolved responders as soon as practicable,
and the participant must receive a notice when doing so will not increase risk to
an affected person.

## Decision and Record

Before a non-emergency decision, responders give the participant a reasonable
opportunity to respond when feasible. They make a good-faith effort to assess the
available information, avoid promising absolute confidentiality, and avoid
requiring affected people to confront the participant. The decision notice states
the finding at an appropriate level of detail, the action and its duration or
conditions, and how to appeal. Responders keep the report, rationale, and outcome
in a restricted record; any public notice omits personal and identifying details.

## Appeals and Review

The sanctioned participant, reporter, or a directly affected participant may
appeal under the [Code of Conduct Appeals process](processes/CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md#code-of-conduct-appeals).
An appeal is due within 14 days of the decision notice and is reviewed by people
who did not make the original decision and have no stake in the outcome. An appeal
does not automatically pause a restriction; the appeal panel may adjust interim
measures when appropriate to protect participants and preserve a fair review.

The community lead and maintainers own this policy and review it annually, and
after a serious enforcement decision. Changes are proposed in a pull request that
touches only the `Governance/` folder.
